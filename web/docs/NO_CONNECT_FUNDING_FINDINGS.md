# No-connect USDC funding findings

**Research date:** 2026-11-17
**Question:** Can a user who does not connect an external wallet or exchange send USDC into an anonymous Helius Ring and receive funds in their email-wallet-controlled betting wallet, with no public traversable funder-to-betting link and no company custody?

## Finding

**Local proof update:** the ordinary SPL receiving-address hop, user-key-signed
anonymous deposit and sponsored 10 BB purchase now pass with real proofs.
See [the experiment and its limits](../../experiments/no-connect-funding/README.md)
and [captured results](../../experiments/no-connect-funding/results.json).
This uses local test keys, not an embedded-wallet integration, and checks
public account separation rather than production whole-lifecycle unlinkability.

**Anonymous Custom Ring ingress supports a hidden recipient; the no-connect UX adds an ordinary public receiving-account hop.** The Brobets local real-proof test shows an external funder can sign a Custom Ring USDC deposit to a hidden player identity without the player signing. The deposit exposes the funder token account, mint, amount, time, fee payer, and Ring program; its recipient is a tag rather than the player's key. The later purchase Ring instruction does not name the funder or private owners. This establishes field-level hiding in the tested custom flow, not whole-lifecycle unlinkability: the test uses a shared fee payer, and its report explicitly flags timing/amount correlation between the public deposit and betting activity. Do not apply the Helius default/public-balance deposit visibility table to this tested anonymous Custom Ring flow.

The selected no-connect shape is: **external sender makes an ordinary SPL USDC transfer to a fresh receiving token account controlled by the user, distinct from the betting wallet; then the email-wallet owner signs a separate Custom Ring deposit from that account to the hidden private identity.** The first hop exposes payer → receiving account; that is acceptable only if the receiving key/account is never publicly associated with the betting wallet. The Ring hop may expose the public source account while hiding the private recipient; it need not hide the funder/source to meet the stated no-traversable-link criterion. The recipient user must authorize the Ring deposit because an exchange or ordinary external transfer cannot sign that Ring-specific instruction. This is not company custody, but it is a second user-authorized step. Timing and amount correlation and app/provider visibility remain.

**Do not infer that Helius offers the complete ingress.** Helius describes on-ramp flows as a provider transferring from its private wallet to the user's private balance, and enterprise docs describe on/off-ramp as integrated providers. They do not document a generic public API/payment-address service that accepts arbitrary SPL transfers from any wallet or exchange and credits an anonymous Ring. Custom Rings are still marked as under audit, with Q4 2026 as a *target* mainnet release. This research does not verify that release or production access.

## What the Helius docs establish

- **The direct anonymous Ring deposit has a distinct signer from its hidden recipient.** In the pinned local Brobets feasibility test, an external funder's SPL account signs `RingNotes::deposit_hidden_from(&funder)` for `owner: player`; the test asserts the funder's account is emptied. The [committed Brobets fixture](https://github.com/alexnham/brobets/blob/2b0652ab9c6f8a771b26d33954d0318183ad502b/experiments/privacy-rings/brobets.rs) and its [feasibility report](https://github.com/alexnham/brobets/blob/2b0652ab9c6f8a771b26d33954d0318183ad502b/web/docs/PRIVATE_FUNDING_FEASIBILITY_REPORT.md) record the public deposit fields and confirm the player signs nothing. The test then asserts the purchase Ring instruction names neither the player nor funder. It ran with real proofs on a local validator using `custom-privacy-rings@cc21e141c65e845e61dca3418f2b1be3815f51f7` and `zolana@b99ba45aa76029f8e89a4004725b9b3a6580a1ab`. The report also records the same public fee payer and timing/amount correlation risk. This proves the custom Ring's hidden-recipient mechanics, not whole-lifecycle unlinkability, production access, or the ordinary public receiving-address hop.
- **Default Ring/public-deposit claims do not generalize to the tested anonymous Custom Ring ingress.** Helius' overview and deposit guide describe public-balance deposits in the permissionless/default flow as exposing sender, destination, asset, and amount. The local anonymous Custom Ring test demonstrates different recipient visibility for that specific custom flow: source fields are public but the private recipient is hidden. ([Overview](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/concepts/overview), [deposit guide](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/guides/deposit), [enterprise Custom Rings](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/integration/enterprise))
- **A plain SPL transfer is not a Ring deposit.** The local test's Ring-specific deposit consumes an SPL source account and requires the source authority's signature. An exchange or external wallet sending an ordinary token transfer to a fresh user-controlled token account does not itself create a private note. The user-controlled account authority must later sign the Ring deposit. Helius SDK deposit examples likewise distinguish the depositor signer, source account, and private recipient owner. ([Deposit guide](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/guides/deposit))
- **Published anonymous on-ramp is provider-mediated, not generic SPL ingress.** In the Helius overview, the on-ramp provider transfers from its private wallet to the user's private balance; sender and recipient are hidden in an anonymous Ring. Fiat payment is handled offchain by that provider. Enterprise docs mention integrated on/off-ramp providers but specify no generic arbitrary-token sender endpoint or drop-in merchant payment address. ([Overview](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/concepts/overview), [enterprise integration](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/integration/enterprise))
- **Self-custody is a supported wallet model.** Helius' wallet integration describes the shielded keypair as signing, nullifier, and viewing keys held by the wallet's key management; its SDK returns instructions for the app/wallet to sign and submit. Keep this user's email-wallet spending key authority distinct from a provider's view/decryption services. The user's assumption that ordinary email-wallet recovery works is taken as given here; shielded recovery was not retested. ([Private wallet integration](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/integration/wallet), [transfer guide](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/guides/transfer))
- **No absolute anonymity or instant finality claim.** Even where Ring fields are encrypted, public timing, the first public payment hop, operational request metadata, and provider/app visibility can correlate activity. Helius explains the on-chain privacy fields, not an absolute anonymity guarantee. Use an observed confirmation and reconciliation state; do not promise a fixed deposit time from these docs.

## Practical decision

| Path | No external wallet connection | User spending ownership | Public-link assessment | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| External wallet signs directly into anonymous Custom Ring | Not an ordinary payment-instructions flow; payer must use a compatible integration to sign the Ring instruction | Recipient can be user's hidden private identity | Source/mint/amount/time remain public and recipient tag is hidden in the test; shared fee payer and timing create correlation | Protocol path proven locally; not the selected no-connect UX |
| External transfer to company wallet, then company deposits | Yes | Company controls public transit funds | Public inbound payment is attributable; company custody introduced | Reject |
| External transfer to fresh user-controlled address distinct from betting wallet; user signs Ring deposit | Yes (no connection), user authorizes second signature | User controls public receiving account and private balance | Public payer→receive-account hop; Ring source remains visible while custom Ring hides private recipient; fee payer/timing/amount may correlate | Local mechanics proof passed; embedded integration and production privacy remain unverified |
| Helius/provider fiat on-ramp to anonymous Ring | No wallet connection may be needed | Ring balance can be user-controlled | Provider is the documented private ingress; requires provider and offchain payment | Provider availability/integration not established; not arbitrary SPL/exchange funding |

An exchange's ordinary withdrawal is similarly an external public transfer; the exchange's hot wallet may be a pooled source and cannot be treated as a user-authorized Ring depositor. Keep exchange refunds/late or excess payment recovery separate from wallet self-custody: returning funds to the observed source may misroute to a shared exchange account. No provider or exchange behavior was contacted or verified.

## Local proof and remaining gaps

1. **Passed locally:** ordinary SPL transfer from a separate external test signer into an associated token account controlled by a distinct receiving key. A sponsor-only attempt to take those tokens fails with SPL OwnerMismatch. No external Brobets/Ring signer is needed for that first transfer.
2. **Passed locally:** the receiving test key authorizes the anonymous deposit; a separate sponsor pays. The real vault buys 10 BB for 11 test USDC in one transaction with the anonymous withdrawal. Landed account lists separate receiving and betting keys; sponsor/setup, timing, amount, logs and instruction-data correlation remain outside that assertion. Embedded-wallet keys and user approval remain untested.
3. Verify Ring spending authority, recovery assumption, zero-company-signing authority, USDC mint/network, SOL/rent payer, transaction status and duplicate/replay handling, and refund behavior for partial/late/excess payment. Test a pooled exchange-style source separately; never refund to it by default.
4. Before calling it viable for production, verify the hosted anonymous Ring, its asset/relay access and the embedded integration; local deposit support is already established. If only provider-mediated on-ramp is supported, assess an actual provider and its custody, payment, refund, and user-authorization terms; do not present a public intermediary or Default Ring as the privacy-preserving fallback.

**Scope note:** This adds a no-connect ingress finding to [the feasibility gate](https://github.com/alexnham/brobets/issues/1) and [combined-release validation](https://github.com/alexnham/brobets/issues/9). It does not change their no-company-custody rule, user's explicit spending authority, accepted USDC/USDC-compatible Solana network requirement, or prohibition on a default confidential Ring fallback. The pinned local real-proof run is evidence for protocol behavior, not devnet/mainnet deployment or a real external-wallet/exchange integration. No network transaction/signature, provider contact, issue, or PR write was made in this research pass.
